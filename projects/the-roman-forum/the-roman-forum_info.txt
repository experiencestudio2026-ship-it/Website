TEXT:
The Roman Forum developed into the political, social and judicial centre of Ancient Rome. Surrounded by temples, basilicas and government buildings, it served as a public space where citizens gathered to discuss political affairs, listen to speeches and participate in civic life. During the Roman Republic, political figures addressed the public from the Rostra, a raised speaking platform, while the nearby Curia housed meetings of the Roman Senate. The Forum illustrates the growing relationship between architecture and political authority, transforming the public square into a monumental space for civic expression and the display of power.
