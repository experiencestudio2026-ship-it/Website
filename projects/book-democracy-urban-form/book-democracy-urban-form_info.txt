TEXT:
This book builds on conversations that took place in 2024 at Harvard GSD, marking the release of Democracy and Urban Form that showcased a series of six lectures and provocations that sociologist Richard Sennett gave under the same name at the GSD in 1981. On October 10, 2024, these provocations continued during a panel that included Richard Sennett, Diane E. Davis, Miguel Robles-Durán, Claire Zimmerman, and Markus Miessen.

“There are Three ways in which I think architectural, but especially democracy of bodies-not a discursive democracy, but a democracy of relationships between human beings in space, themselves. That’s the basis for all my urbanism, the notion of making people aware of themselves as one among many, which I think cities can do, and which produces a kind of democracy of the body.” (Richard Sennett, p. 26)

“It is a profound principle of how right to the city works, the assumption that through experience people develop the knowledge of how to use the city. This vision on a larger scale allows for people to reorient themselves to things that are not safe, learning that they are one among many, and to gradually develop a kind of urban competency. This urban competency can be applied to both space and people. Aldo’s principle about children learning how to play in a city can also be approached in the way adults deal with each other in the street and within institutions.” (Richard Sennett, p. 28)
