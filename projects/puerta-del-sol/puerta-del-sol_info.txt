TITLE: Puerta del Sol
LOCATION: Madrid, Spain
AUTHOR: Lucio del Valle, Juan Rivera and José Morer (19th-century transformation)
YEAR: 1857–1862, latest renovation 2023
CONTRIBUTED BY: Claudia Gómez Coronado
SIZE: approx. 12,900 m²
CREDITS: El Confidencial; Linazasoro & Sánchez

TEXT:
Puerta del Sol is one of Madrid's most emblematic public spaces. Its semicircular morphology connects several major streets, creating a central point of circulation and social interaction. The square represents Spain's geographical and symbolic centre, marked by Kilometre Zero. Its latest renovation prioritizes pedestrian movement and creates a large, uninterrupted public space.

On 15 May 2011, Puerta del Sol became the epicentre of the Indignados Movement (15M), protesting against political corruption, economic inequality and the lack of democratic representation. Thousands of citizens occupied the square for several weeks, transforming it into a temporary self-organized city with assemblies, libraries and communal facilities. The occupation demonstrated how public space can become an instrument of direct democracy and collective political expression.
